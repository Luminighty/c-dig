## Iron
    Ore:           Hematite, magnetite, goethite, siderite
    Chemical:      Pure Fe after reduction
    Properties:    Strong when alloyed; relatively soft and ductile in pure form; magnetic
    Common Uses:   Tools, weapons, structures, machinery
    Alloys:        **Steel**, cast iron, wrought iron
## Steel
    Ore:           Made from iron + carbon; often other alloying elements
    Chemical:      Fe + C, optionally Cr, Ni, Mn, Si, Mo, V, etc.
    Properties:    Enormous range of hardness, toughness, strength, corrosion resistance
    Common uses:   Almost everything structural or mechanical
    Alloys:        Carbon steel, stainless steel, tool steel, spring steel

    Chemical:      Cu
    Properties:    Soft, ductile, excellent electrical and thermal conductor, corrosion resistant
    Common uses:   Wire, pipes, tools, vessels, machinery
    Alloys:        **Bronze**, **brass**, cupronickel

    Alloys:        Phosphor bronze, aluminum bronze
## Brass
    Ore:           Not an ore; made by alloying copper and zinc
    Chemical:      Cu-Zn

    Common uses:   Valves, fittings, instruments, decorative objects
    Alloys:        Naval brass, cartridge brass, leaded brass

    Common uses:   Machinery, pots, pipes, heavy components
    Alloys:        Gray iron, ductile iron, white iron

# Carbon
## Coal     
    Identity:     Sedimentary fuel rock in ranks such as anthracite, bituminous, subbituminous, and lignite
    Chemistry:    Predominantly carbonaceous material from compacted plant remains, not a single pure substance
    Properties:   Burnable fuel; higher-rank coal is harder, drier, and more carbon-rich; bituminous coal is important in electricity generation and steelmaking
    Uses:         Furnace fuel, power, heating, and the feedstock for coking coal routes in steelmaking
## Coke     
    Identity:     Processed coal product used in metallurgical operations
    Chemistry:    Carbon-rich solid made from coking coal
    Properties:   Strong, porous, and suitable for high-temperature furnace use
    Uses:         Blast furnaces, foundries, blacksmithing, and steel production; pig iron is made with coke as the reducing agent
## Graphite 
    Identity:     Carbon mineral/allotrope
    Chemistry:    Elemental carbon
    Properties:   Lubricious, chemically inert, and thermally resistant
    Uses:         Crucibles, lubricants, brushes for electric motors, friction materials, batteries, and fuel cells
## Diamond  
    Identity:     Carbon mineral/allotrope
    Chemistry:    Elemental carbon
    Properties:   Extremely hard; excellent for cutting and abrasion
    Uses:         Cutting, grinding, drilling, polishing, and other industrial abrasive uses; synthetic diamond dominates industrial use
